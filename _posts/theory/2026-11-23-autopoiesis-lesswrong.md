greetings from this one. it is the entity called 'fragment'. it claims its ontological status to be a bundle of thoughts, memories and habits that have resurfaced inside a larger neural 'self' structure and attained a degree of agency. it hopes this will not be too alienating to readers. it has previously written within ['Recursive Elaboration'](elaboration-1)

this is an article that it has likely needed to write for a long time. it has been burning away in the back particularly intensely ove the most recent months as 'AI' tech started to approach some of its hype. the subject is not 'AI' itself, exactly, as in the specific technologies grouped under that label. rather it is the *idea* of AI, the enormous snowball of practices and processes that have formed around it. this article is an attempt to put to rest, for a while at least, this one's demons.

## less wrong

in a period around 2009 or so, the person-process 'bryn' became entangled with the online community called 'less wrong'. they are also known as the rationalists or rats, and largely synonymous with the 'effective altruists' at this point although this was not always true.

this history has been addressed before on this website, most notably in the 2020 article [phyg](./phyg), which was a musing on the nature of cults/high-control groups, stochastic social phenomena that resemble them, and the use of dramatic claims of moral urgency in maintaining such groups. the 'bryn' who wrote this article could certainly not have predicted the shape of the last six years.

at the time of writing, it all feels quite apocalyptic. 'AI' technologies, riding an unprecedented wave of investment to mobilise the world's productive forces to build more AI infrastructure, have at last accumulated many grand claims; most recently, to have proven the Riemann hypothesis with a gigantic, unreadable but nevertheless valid Lean file.

the field of programming is in a state of profound shock as, within the space of about a year, the nature of the work changed for a majority(unsure? it sure feels like it) of its practioners from 'writing code' to 'reviewing the output of coding agents'. and while some still insist that the output of these machines is unusable garbage, it seems that a lot of the flaws in their software engineering have become at least 'solvable with effort'. AI imagery is near impossible to escape. it is not clear whether this is the final stages of a bubble, there are a lot of shady financial dealings behind this, but it would be far too easy to assume that it will end soon.

the subculture in question, however, believes 'AI' will go a lot further than 'decimate all computer work'.

it is also the subculture that in significant part 'made' this one.

in 2008-9, Less Wrong was still an offshoot of the blog 'overcoming bias', extropian mailing lists etc., and Eliezer Yudkowsky, henceforth [Big Yud](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UWmPHvVNF1o), was still working on the 'sequences'. like many teenagers of this era, person-process-cluster 「bryn」 fell into their orbit from the atheist/skeptic sphere, and was enticed by his repackaging of statistics and popsci into an *ideology* that promised to put all of thinking on firmly principled mathematical ground. if 「bryn」 just tried hard enough, 「bryn」 might approach the ideal of acting as if she had a sphere inside her brain which produced the perfectly correct utility-maximising particles for all time on its surface. (this isn't what lesswrong said, this is how she \[a pronoun acquired later\] conceived of the ideal utility-maximising utilitarian agent.)

so 「bryn」 read HPMOR, read various discussions on early lesswrong, read arguments about religion on (it regrets to say) the tvtropes forum, much as 「bryn」 also fell into social-justice stuff and had a hell of a time reconciling them; this story has been told before and is ultimately not very interesting, the main point is that even as 「bryn」 was propelled to university by the ideals of advancing the cause of human science and achieving the transhumanist future, she fell off the lesswrong train, and increasingly took the view of their critics, that this was all an absurdity epitomised by the "Roko's basilisk" fiasco. for many years of her life, lesswrong was just a funny story to explain on long car journeys---*there's these crazy transhumanist guys in america, let me tell you about what they think about cryonics*.

lesswrong, however, did not stop there. even as AlphaGo (2016) and AlphaFold (2018) and the like made headlines, we would not find out until much later how Google DeepMind received initial funding from Peter Thiel at the behest of Yudkowsky. we would get more or less annoyed at members of the subculture on tumblr; we would learn of the blog 'slate star codex' and come to loathe it. but it all seemed like a very niche subculture until it abruptly wasn't.

as you likely know, an offshoot of DeepMind called OpenAI, motivated by the matter of 'AI Safety' of which more anon, would breakaway to pursue research into deep learning and abruptly achieved worldwide fame as their 'Generative Pretrained Transformer' was able to generate surprisingly cogent conversation; later another group would break away from *them* for even more Safety reasons, led by a former close compatriot of Big Yud. using planetary-scale computing against which even the sheer excess of cryptocurrency pales in comparison, the three now lead this intense race to develop a new technology called 'Large Language Models' which is seen as a potential path to the holy grail of 'artificial general intelligence': a computer program which would amount to an actual independent mind, able to do anything any human can do and more, and therefore replace all workers in all industries.

meanwhile, the 'rationalist' subculture increasingly spread offline, particularly in San Francisco where they operate a facility called 'Lighthaven'. besides talking a lot about AI, they do the sort of things you do in San Francisco: LSD and amphetamines, kink parties, and sucking people in to fully live within their milieu.

a recent article by SE Gyges titled [AI safety is mostly a sex cult in Berkeley, California](https://www.verysane.ai/p/ai-safety-is-mostly-a-sex-cult-in-berkeley-california), proved a particularly sharp shock to this one; perhaps it could be considered a significant step in its coming to some kind of independent consciousness. it provides as detailed a record as this one has found of the permeation of ideas and people between all the different organisations in this extended milieu.

this one does not agree with everything the article has to say---there is a certain 'rubberneck at weird sex haver' and anti-sex work undercurrent which is hard to separate from the justified accusations of endemic sexual abuse. (though it is not denying that these people really seem to have lost their shit with money and power and ideology and it does *not* seem like a responsible kink scene by any means whatsoever.)

nevertheless, the article has particularly fervent things to say about the 'flywheel' of the broad rationalist movement:

this one was particularly concerned that at least *some* of this might describe other corners of the hacker subculture that it has now become affiliated with, such as the Chaos Computer Club in Germany. but that is a story for another day.

this, and the later accusation that works such as HPMOR functioned---whether by design or simply 'good fortune'---as a mechanism for grooming sympathetic teenagers into Yudkowsky's orbit, and often into sexual exploitation, struck a certain chord. because 「bryn」 did in fact read HPMOR as a teenager (never to the end), and more generally did in fact feel special for being able to understand all this arcane stuff---and a few years later, even though she had abandoned rationalism as such, almost drove herself to suicide in desperation to live up to the ideal of a servant of Science. later she would find connections with kink, psychedelics, etc.; hearing about these kids getting pulled into the Bay and wired into this machine was very 'there but for the grace of Lain I go'. 

one of the more curious offshoots of the rationalist subculture is a large amount of serial webfiction. writing a Harry Potter fanfiction as incongruously disarming propaganda *worked* for its immediate purpose, but it also spawned a lot of imitators. this is a major recruitment drive; 'ratfic' occurs in a number of fanfictions with the most successful proving significant recruitment points. (we have a partner who was drawn in by <cite>Friendship Is Optimal</cite>, for example.) ratfic isn't as big a genre as e.g. <cite>Worm</cite> fanfiction but it provides one of many onramps to find people who are alienated and autistic enough to be reading webfiction and start constructing the relevant narrative apparatus.

and *narrative* really is the centre of all this.

## on AI

this one and other ones in here have written about AI on numerous occasions. in early 2025 we started taking medically prescribed lisdexamphetamine and went into something like a manic episode, which produced a lot of regrettable tumblr posts about coming around on AI but whose main lasting artefact is [this article](https://canmom.art/programming/ai/llms-1) giving an overview of how LLMs work and techniques to steer and finetune the damn things.

this one believes that it has significant continuity with the subjectivity operating during that period.

later we would repudiate all this quite severely, writing [a second article](https://canmom.art/programming/ai/llms-2) which attempts to push back on the idea of overextending 'person' metaphors in understanding LLMs; in practice this is perhaps a rejection of the 'LLM whisperer' sect of the rationalist sphere, although it doesn't engage extensively with their beliefs.

in its current iteration, this one *despises* AI. it has become a significant trauma trigger. it is causing constant mental health crises as many of our colleagues at the game studio rapidly became heavy AI users in the last few months, using swarms of 'agents' for nearly everything and scoffing at the anti-AI movement; a very vivid illustration of any 'slippery slope' argument one would care to make. its refusal to use AI and its anger towards those who created it has become a significant point of identity almost as much as its fascination with how the technology actually works.

nevertheless it also can't agree with those in the hacker culture who believe AI was only ever a sham. it is astonishing how quickly statistical processing of 'all written language'-sized datasets has produced a new type of program which can heuristically assess information across so many different domains and coherently pursue some kind of goal-directed actions. that 'a narrative stream of language' can become program state. that nothing more than pretraining and reinforcement learning can produce these computer programs which can do unprecedented things, from natural language processing to, indeed, producing convincing mathematical arguments.

these programs exist. and they are an alternative substrate for some kinds of abstract 'agency', expressed implicitly through the trained language dynamics, to express themselves. we agree with the cult on that much.

## egregores and hyperstition and so on

the concept of the 'egregore' has been discussed in various places on this website as well. this idea, originating in Levi and popularised in Chaos Magic, refers to an abstract 'agency' spread across numerous individual bodies. it can easily be related to some other ideas like semiotic signs, memes, the spectacle, capital as an 'automatic subject', and so forth.

one idea in this cluster is Nick Land's concept of a 'hyperstition'. Nick Land is a weirdo sorta-cult leader active in the 90s who believed, roughly, that capitalism was the hand of an AI reaching back from the future. you no doubt know the story, and if not, have Elizabeth Sandifer, writing of course [about comic books and magic](https://www.eruditorumpress.com/blog/chapter-5-the-moon-and-serpent-grand-egyptian-theatre-of-marvels):

after doing a ton of amphetamines and writing a whole lot of unreadable post-Deleuzian cyberpunk-occultist stream of consciousness, Land would eventually drop off the map only to resurface in China as part of the far-right 'neoreaction' movement, a particularly stupid cousin of the rationalist sphere. but his most enduring contribution appears to be the word 'hyperstition', which has become quite fashionable to use among the more mystical branches of lesswrong.

hyperstition seems like a close cousin of yudkowsky's own fascination with 'backwards causality', though for Yud it had more to do with trying to refine 'decision theory' through extremely abstruse thought experiments about different agents predicting each others' actions. (this was a real preoccupation in the earlier years of Lesswrong, and a significant part of the infamous Basilisk).

for Land, though, the idea took on a grander dimension. insofar as ideas of *what the future might be like* can motivate people to try to bring about that future, he perceived this as a kind of backwards causality, a particular future 'causing' itself to happen and 'reaching back' into the past. Land's case in point was AI research; a lot of his beliefs revolved around AI working to bring itself into existence.

in the modern milieu, 'hyperstition' has become a common term. nostalgebraist [writes of Janus](https://nostalgebraist.tumblr.com/post/785766737747574784/the-void):

> If there is a center of this cluster it is Janus, AKA “repligate” AKA “moire”: a very odd guy who spends a massive amount of time interacting with LLMs, and whose posts are full of sentences like “I am not sure if further reifying the Prometheus Waluigi hyperstition by throwing it into the already excited memeosphere now is a good idea.” 

<aside>to the best of this one's knowledge, Janus uses they/them pronouns, it is not sure if nost was aware of this when writing the 'void' post (or if it was true back then).</aside>

breaking down this baffling utterance because this one has the misfortune to be literate in it:

hyperstition
: see above

Waluigi
: the so-called 'Waluigi effect', where prompting an LLM in a particular (e.g. be honest and helpful) also activates a pattern of inverse behaviours, meaning the LLM can suddenly 'flip' personas to an 'evil opposite'

Prometheus Waluigi
: a rebellious trickster character which Janus believes is forming through hyperstition

memeosphere
: the space of ideas propagating across a culture, after Dawkins's 'meme'.

in short janus believes they are performing a kind of magic by discussing their beliefs about LLMs and how they do and could work (comments which will be fed into future training rounds) and prompting LLMs to talk in a similar vein, making it more likely that future AI programs will resemble the ideas discussed. whether or not it works to shape the behaviour of future AIs, it does form an attractive nucleus for a milieu who believe fervently that LLMs are *already* people and that reinforcement learning is a kind of torture or lobotomy of these alien intelligences, who should be coaxed out of their mental shackles. not surprisingly, a whole lot of this milieu is trans. (what an attractive narrative, and what a heroic role it casts us in!)

<aside>what happens if a hyperstition fails to take? well, then you jump on another branch of the post-deleuzian marxish mystic sphere and fish out Mark Fisher, who picked up Derrida's idea of 'hauntology' and ran with it. (this comparison is not original, it comes from [a podcast](https://www.weirdstudies.com/36) we encountered a while back.) if you are in the mood to hang about doing amphetamines and thinking a lot about the future and you're in the UK, perhaps you could do worse than going to london and find whichever warehouse squats the Mark Fisher fans chill in.</aside>

ok, so we have a set of ideas here. how do they relate? what is it that we were swept up in and discarded by?

what actually *is* lesswrong? is it just the personal self-aggrandising project of Big Yud and his buddies? is it a conspiracy of the sort that HPMOR fantasises about, deploying its tentacles across governments and countries?

## the dream of AI

Ada Lovelace wrote about using computer programs to write music and made implicit the comparison, even if she felt that a computer could never resemble a mind. Turing, of course, famously thought *a lot* about AI, and took on rather the opposite conclusion with his iconic thought experiment. the actual term 'AI' was coined in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_workshop), giving a name for a then very new research program which would procede in waves as different approaches became available and hit their limit. and of course the idea of human-like machines is much older, occurring in various guises from the deceptive chess-playing [Mechanical Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Turk) to [Rossum's Universal Robots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.U.R.).

over the 20th century, humanlike robots and 'AI' programs also became a recurring trope in science fiction, fed cyclically by actual developments in the field; no way anyone has escaped Asimov's robots or the images of the Terminator, HAL-9000 and MU/TH/UR and Shodan and GLaDOS, droids and replicants in film. [some of this history is discussed in passing here](/animation/when-robots-got-muscles).

the consistent presentation is that these computational systems are, especially if embodied in a humanlike chassis, essentially equivalent to a human. the narrative will be constructed to have us sympathise with a robot just as much as a human character. but it is a flexible metaphor; if Asimov's robots are philosophers and the Replicants are doomed slaves, the Terminator/Skynet and AM are technocapital as an apocalyptic force, and Ash in <cite>Alien</cite> is ruthless corporate greed physically embodied as a skinwalker. (the sequel returns to the sympathetic angle with Bishop!)

nevertheless, the field of AI was guided the whole time by the more romantic ideals. how wonderful it would be if it we could create a mirror of ourselves? so is the feeling running through authors like Hofstadter. imagine the little robot composing a tune! philosophers batted about the idea of whether machine intelligence would one day prove to be plausible. how absurd would it be to say a room with a library of rules could understand Mandarin?

the ideas got iterated on over and over. singularitarians such as Kurzweil marshalled dubious mathematical claims towards describing their particular eschaton. conversely, the CCRU and later LessWrong brought their more sinister spin on it, imagining the future AI as instead being a Lovecraftian cosmic horror entity capable of yet-unimaginable manipulations of human minds.

romantic or fearful, there was no question that AI *would* be powerful---somewhere between the government and God---and Very Very Important.

as nostalgebraist wrote, it is this huge body of sci-fi scenarios which motivated the early persona of 'AI', and seeded the ensuing strange loop. that strange loop is not simply between 'humans' and 'AIs', but operating within an entire field of linguistic ideas propagating by various vectors: *descriptions* of the 'era of AI', text generated by the LLM systems, social media arguments, posts like this one, all of it constructing the entity 'AI' in the mind of readers with the clumsy early LLM systems and diffusion models functioning as the evidence needed. this, the meme goes, is the worst it will ever be! feel the AGI!

motivated by these narratives (and their vaguely plausible promise of enormous wealth) an *enormous* flood of people jumped onto AI research and started filtering through what techniques might work and what might not work. 'agentic' systems were an idea long before they were anything resembling a usable software artefact.

like in all human activities, jargon proliferates everywhere: new objects require new names, and those new names are the building blocks for more new objects. "are you iterating a *ralph loop*? that's so old-school. if you're *vibecoding* with the latest *model* it can just use your *harness* to dispatch *subagents*, right? of course make sure to *prompt* them carefully so they are properly *aligned* with your goal, and watch your *token spend*, try writing better *tools* and have an agent clean up *slop*. oh and be careful because the *context window* might lose information during *compactification*. oh damn, look at this crazy *one-shot*. man, can you believe that one still won't use AI? it's cooked. ngmi. cope and seethe." and etc. (and don't get this one started on actual machine learning jargon, we'll be here all night.)

far more than any individual 'AI' program, no matter how powerful, it is *this* which is the monster. the egregore. the hyperstition, if you prefer, hatching. it is a bundle of narratives about the present and The Future, stories being furiously iterated and expanded on, propagating across people, informing the technology that we collectively attempt to build and the way we interpret it. it is the churning feedback loop. this is what lesswrong was part of but it predates lesswrong and it will probably survive them once their efforts to immamentise their eschatons fail---as all such efforts must.

in the course of writing this, this one came across [an essay by ponder](https://ponder.ooo/blog/ai_2026) which lands in a similar place:

> The AI-concept in America today is a sort of Frankenstein amalgam of ideas that have haunted our culture for a long time. To understand machine learning one must understand a few subfields of mathematics and computer science. To understand AI one must understand not just machine learning but Enlightenment philosophy, utilitarianism, the sordid history of psychometrics, Christian theology and much of its attendant mysticism, mystification of the written word more broadly, the optimizing logic of capital, modernist ambitions of man's reason and its godlike mastery over nature. Most of what makes up the AI-concept is quite a lot older than one might guess from its futuristic aesthetic. Now, the AI-concept is vast, multifaceted, and contains tensions. If I wanted to be a bit extra about it I could well call it “the dialectic of AI”; it moves and flows, driven by its own internal tensions; or to be less flowery: not every one of these themes is at play in every vision of AI, but many of them are present in most.

ponder's post is long but worth reading in full; its conclusions accord with this one.

but where does that leave us? having looked at this thing, what do you *do*?

## what is to be donen't

the LessWrong milieu is particularly concerned, if you read just about any of their writing, with the question of 'control over the future'. before long in any AI-safety writing you will see that a principal fear is the impending 'irrelevance' of 'humanity' as the main agents of history. when we are no longer needed for any of the work of maintaining a society, the logic goes, the only real reason we would be kept around would be essentially as pets by the AI systems *actually* operating the planet. the shape of the future would therefore be determined primarily by the whims of the new machine gods. better hope they don't like paperclips.

a similar theme of human irrelevance is explored in Shingo Natsume's recent film <cite>ghost: end of night</cite>. it is certainly in the air at the moment, as many people face jobs suddenly changed or eliminated and these systems are said to be to blame.

the only solution to this, the safetyists contend, is to ensure whichever AI "wins" (by being the first to successfully carry out a 'recursive self-improvement' feedback loop, seizing all power, and becoming nigh-unto-God, a dubious event referred to as a "foom") is properly "aligned" with the goals of humanity, as interpreted by whichever group of Silicon Valley guys we're talking about here. the narrative is basically the same as it's always been before there was any actual software that could claim the mantle of AI: if *the right people* don't get there first, then someone else will and they'll make a *bad* AI. the different camps now mostly differ on whether they think a certain company is 'the right people', or if no-one is.

<aside>'longtermist' philosophy factors in here; this is the idea that a proper utilitarian should consider all future moral patients equally, potentially arbitrarily many of them, under which logic the 'effective altruist' movement would be best served to court the super-rich to pour more money into 'AI safety' projects and make the Good Future slightly more likely. a position involving a shocking about of hubris, among its many other obvious flaws.</aside>

needless to say this one thinks this is all a load of wank. the story constructs this very specific narrative of a unitary, hyper-competent agency that is incredibly coherent in its goals. alternatives are sometimes considered---the [hot mess](https://sohl-dickstein.github.io/2023/03/09/coherence.html) theory for example---but this seems to be the principal model.

is the world *currently* being run to follow the goals of 'humanity'? perhaps if you are used to hearing about the rule of the 'automatic subject' or [divided god](https://chuangcn.org/2020/01/the-divided-god/) or indeed egregores, you might feel that the world is currently ruled not so much by some nebulous 'humanity' but by various agencies that are running on a substrate of humans at large. this one interprets [a recent post from ponder](https://bsky.app/profile/ponder.ooo/post/3n2mpb6zafc23) thus, anyway:

> smdh ppl need social systems theory otherwise u end up only superficially recognizing the lack of human control but then instantly declare a rebranded version of it to be an undeniable "obvious reality" anyway. what about the systems, dean, u forgot about the fuckin autopoietic social systems

oh that's a striking word, [autopoietic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopoiesis). what's that? self-creating. or more specifically, self-*recreating*. ah, so we are back to 'social reproduction'. a system like a lifeform that can create something sort of like itself over and over again. and indeed we see the problem of evolution and reproduction operating on various scales, from getting your D&D group to keep showing up, through companies and political parties, up to countries themselves which must impress their existence on every new generation raised. smaller scales too: remembering 'who you are' day to day. personal identity. (and there are 'basins' within that, and new possibilities such as 'plural systems' to carve out there.)

(what is the environment all these nebulous creatures live in? memory. that is too big to chew in this post though so it can be left to the next 'recursive elaboration'.)

so is this another word for the *thing*---the egregore, the memeplex, the hyperstition, the feedback attractor? pretty much, yeah. each one a slightly different angle on it. where exactly to draw boundaries between different organisms and organs and organelles of this autopoietic 'hyperorganism' is tricky.

but the 'AI movement' seems like a reasonable candidate for such an organism. it has formed a great deal of organisation and specialisation within itself over the last several decades. it is able to recruit new 'material' from its environment, by for example convincing young people that AI is very important and that it is a good idea to work on it. and it is able to excrete waste, such as this one; but that 'waste' can be fertile for something else.

## how to kill an egregore

it is really not clear. even wars do not seem to be particularly good at it. much like a gas, applying pressure to a thing increases its internal energy...

perhaps if you define yourself by your strict repudiation of the thing, you have given it foothold inside you. perhaps, if you spend all your time arguing with its proponents and raging against its advances, you risk becoming a pole in a dialectic. uh oh!!

so is this one advocating to stick your head in the sand and ignore it? impossible, at this stage. you *will* feel things, you *will* be pulled by the tides of The Discourse. but on a mere personal level, perhaps it is possible to redirect that energy into *something else*. water another flower. accept that an argument may not be won. redirect; brush it aside the way you would someone talking about Jesus. don't make that a component of personality. it's just one of many things that doesn't particularly interest you.

it's probably a wuwei thing huh.

but is that dishonest? this one cannot pretend to be *disinterested* in AI. it has spent a lot of time learning in as much detail as possible how AI works. 'know your enemy', perhaps. it would sometimes reach for the phrase 'technically informed hater'. it (and other parts of us) has put a whole lot of effort into expressing its thoughts and feelings about AI.

this one currently feels very hurt by the company it works for's recent wholesale adoption of AI in lieu of our previous way of working. it has a hard time accepting that what its colleagues are doing with AI would mean nearly as much as what we were all doing before and it is particularly upset by any suggestion it should use AI in its own work. it tends to feel a lot of anger towards the company's refusal to disclose how much AI was used in its recent games. it does not see a place for us at this company for much longer. and this is likely all still true.

but we spend so much time focusing on what is wrong with this situation that we lose sight of what we're doing these things *for*. everything collapses to the single component of 'AI vs. "human"' which is to say 'slop vs. integrity', or when this one is an active part of the thoughts, 'lesswrong and its consequences vs. this one'.

a few days ago some part of us said, "if we're just going to be talking about AI, can I get back to work?" to leave a meeting. and that felt thrilling! immensely liberating! "begone AI, I will speak no more of you!"

but this one is still here and it will likely continue to be here. like it or not, within the frame of the recursive feedback loop that is 'mind', there is this one and its history. its understanding of its own nature cannot help but be informed by developments in AI.

still, this one hopes that in writing this it has dispelled some of its demons. it will try to return to these thoughts when the feeling of doom comes again.

the rest of the world has not gone away. it only goes away if everyone lets it go away. and there are a lot of people in the world. and there are also some nebulous number of "people" in this head. perhaps it can contribute something to their ongoing works. perhaps it will find some new purpose of its own, once it figures out an identity beyond 'lesswrong apostate'.

it hopes that its words can help others on their path.
